How the forecasts work

Each series is fitted three ways over the years you choose, then the best fit is picked by AICc on log-scale residuals (fewer parameters win ties).

  • Linear: y = a + b·t. Fits steady additions (the same number more each year).
  • Exponential: ln y = a + b·t, so y = e^a·e^(b·t). Fits compounding growth. Doubling time = ln 2 / b.
  • Logistic (S-curve): y = K / (1 + e^(−r(t − t₀))). Fits growth that saturates at a ceiling K. Many real exponentials turn into this eventually; the hard part is that the data rarely shows K until you're past the midpoint.

The band. The shaded area is an 80% prediction interval from the fit's residuals, widened with distance from the data (±1.28·s·√(1 + 1/n + (t−t̄)²/Sₜₜ)). It only reflects how noisy the past was. It does not know about regime changes, new reporting rules, or the next WannaCry.

Provenance. Every reported value comes from the source named under its chart, and every series was re-checked against its sources by an independent pass on 2026-10-20 (see VERIFICATION.md in the repository). Values that could not be verified are left blank and never estimated by this board. Some values are the source's own estimates or are read from a source's chart; their notes say so. Derived series are computed from verified series and say so. Analyst notes are interpretation and are labeled as such. Event dates are verified; one relies on secondary reporting and is marked.

Year-to-date values. A partial year is never fitted, but it does set a floor: the year’s final total cannot be lower than its year-to-date count, so the band’s lower edge is raised to it. If the year-to-date count is already above the band, the outlook says so plainly: the trend forecast for that year has been contradicted by actuals.

The safe horizon. Forecasts stop at min(5, ⌊n/3⌋) years past the last full year of data, where n is the number of years fitted. Past that, the board shows nothing rather than false precision.

Bets and calibration. A bet is a probability you commit to before the answer is known. Resolved bets are scored with the Brier score, mean((p − outcome)²): 0 is perfect, 0.25 is what always saying 50% earns. For series-threshold bets the board also shows the model's own probability, so you can see where your judgment disagrees with the trend line, and later, who was right.

Reading it like a TAF. The outlook box borrows the aviation terminal forecast format: a validity window, a most-likely value, and a probability group. Treat it the way you'd treat a TAF issued for next year: useful for planning, never a go/no-go by itself.
